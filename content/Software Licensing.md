This is an incoherent list of things I would eventually like to touch on...

Switch to using [0BSD]() for everything as a close approximation of public domain (which is a [legal mess](https://guides.library.cornell.edu/copyright/publicdomain) in the US, but also globally). Inspired by a burntsushi's take and further discussion on HN https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=24939162 and see this for the breakdown of why not CC0 https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39807310 which, due to the patent issue, some projects have stepped away from accepting (like Fedora). It's a bit of a weird argument due to implicit versus explicit, but it sounds like a realistic concern due to patent trolls. The reason for not MIT-0 would be that it's not as universally accepted...for example, it's not on Google's "unencumbered" list: https://opensource.google/documentation/reference/thirdparty/licenses

I've always been on the more permissive side of the fence for open source (I prefer MIT/BSD/ISC rather than copyleft like GPL). I don't really care about the attribution aspect (it's nice, but shouldn't be required), I can't imagine ever actually wanting/needing to pursue any legal repercussions for violators, and my goal is the sharing itself not personal profit. Explicitly allow AI training on my data is also an advocacy thing.
